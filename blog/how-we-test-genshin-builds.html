How We Test Genshin Builds Before Recommending Them

By Song · September 1, 2026 · Editorial methodology

A build guide should do more than name a five-star weapon and a popular team. Most players are making decisions with incomplete artifacts, limited resin, and a roster that does not match a showcase video. Our goal is to explain why a recommendation works, how much it matters, and what to use when the ideal option is unavailable.

Short version: we compare a build against a realistic baseline, test the complete rotation rather than one screenshot, and publish the trade-offs. A recommendation is not a promise of a fixed damage number.

1. We define the player question first

Before comparing equipment, we write down the decision the page is meant to help with. “Best weapon” is too broad. A useful question is closer to: “Should a low-spend player use a craftable weapon or spend wishes for this character’s signature weapon?” That question changes the criteria: availability, energy needs, refinement cost, team requirements, and the value of the account’s existing resources all matter.

2. We separate facts from recommendations

Character talents, weapon passives, artifact effects, and domain schedules are facts that can be checked. Team rankings and pull advice are judgments. In our drafts, we keep those categories separate so a reader can tell what is an in-game rule and what is our interpretation.

When a mechanic depends on a patch, we include the relevant version in the title or body. When information is a prediction or leak, it must be labeled clearly. We do not use a leak as evidence for a current build recommendation.

3. We use a complete rotation, not a damage screenshot

A showcase screenshot can hide setup time, food buffs, five-star constellations, perfect artifacts, or repeated retries. For a team comparison, we look at the full loop: how the team applies elements, creates particles, refreshes buffs, and returns to the main damage window. A team that produces a larger single hit but misses its burst on the next rotation may feel worse in actual Abyss play.

We also ask whether the rotation is usable on mobile, whether it depends on strict enemy positioning, and whether a missed dash or interrupted skill makes the team collapse. These details are part of the recommendation, not optional footnotes.

4. We compare realistic baselines

Our baseline is not a perfect account. We prefer a serviceable artifact set, a reasonable main-stat setup, and weapons a player can actually obtain. The exact numbers vary with buffs and enemy resistance, so we use numbers to understand direction rather than to claim a universal result.

  • Weapon: compare signature, common gacha, battle-pass, craftable, and free options where they serve different players.
  • Artifacts: explain the important set effect and stat threshold before discussing small substat gains.
  • Team: give at least one alternative that preserves the core reaction or energy loop.
  • Rotation: show the order of skills and the reason for that order.

5. We explain when the advice changes

There is rarely one best answer for every account. An energy-recharge weapon can beat a higher-damage option when a burst is not ready on cooldown. An attack buffer can be less valuable in a team already saturated with attack bonuses. A character that is excellent for Abyss may be inconvenient for exploration because the rotation requires stationary targets.

Every recommendation should include these “use this instead when…” conditions. They are often more useful than moving a character from one tier-list letter to another.

6. We show the limits of the test

Our tests cannot represent every account, platform, ping condition, enemy wave, constellation level, or artifact inventory. We say so when those variables could change the result. If a page contains an estimate, the estimate is labeled as an estimate; if a value is patch-sensitive, the reader is pointed to the official source or the game client for confirmation.

What readers can expect from our guides

The result should be a decision aid: a clear recommendation, a reason, an accessible alternative, and a practical rotation. If a page does not add that context, it needs more editing before it is ready. This is also why we revise older guides instead of treating publication as the end of the work.
