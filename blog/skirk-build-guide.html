Skirk Build Guide: Mechanics and Account Planning

Corrected September 5, 2026 · Source-based guide
Home › Blog › Skirk Build Guide
Skirk Build Build Planning Cryo Guide

Start a Skirk build by choosing how you want to play her and checking which teammates you already own. The decisions below separate basic mechanics from a practical spending checklist.

Correction — September 5, 2026: The previous article contained incorrect skill, equipment, and material information. Those sections have been replaced. This is a source-based guide, not a report of our own gameplay tests. The reference guide covers Version 5.7; this page does not claim a current-patch damage ranking.

Mechanics that change your build

Skirk uses Serpent's Subtlety instead of Energy for her Burst. Energy Recharge therefore does not help charge it. Her Tap Skill enables her on-field attack state; Hold Skill supports a Burst-focused approach. Prioritize Skill levels for on-field play and Burst levels for quickswap. Her enhanced attacks scale with Skill, not the Normal Attack talent.

Equipment and team starting points

Azurelight is her signature sword. Finale of the Deep is a craftable alternative when healing enables its passive. Finale of the Deep Galleries is a general-purpose artifact option; Marechaussee Hunter depends on maintaining its stacks and suits particular Furina on-field setups. Start with ATK% sands, a Cryo or ATK% goblet, and a CRIT circlet; check total CRIT Rate before replacing pieces.

Use Hydro and Cryo teammates to support her passives. Escoffier and Furina are notable partners; Furina teams need healing. These mechanics and equipment notes are summarized from KQM's Skirk Quick Guide and its credited theorycrafting team.

Make a plan with your existing account

Before farming, write down your intended four-character team, the weapon you can equip today, and the talent you intend to raise next. Leave an empty slot visible if you cannot yet fill a role. This makes the cost of a proposed build easier to judge than copying four portraits from a showcase.

  • Already own usable equipment? Save that build as a baseline. Compare a new item against it before spending more materials.
  • Considering a new weapon? List the billets, levels, refinement materials, and teammates it requires. Compare that total commitment with improving the weapon you already have.
  • Planning two teams? Write both rosters side by side. Assign each shared support to one team before deciding that a new damage dealer fills the remaining gap.

A practice-session worksheet

Choose one encounter you can repeat. Record your team, equipment, talent levels, and whether you finish the attempt. On the next attempt, change only one thing. Note interruptions, missed attacks, and time spent repositioning as well as the result. A single large damage number does not describe how the whole attempt went.

Use those notes to choose your next upgrade. If the comparison is unclear, keep the current equipment and repeat the same encounter. This worksheet is a suggested way to evaluate your account; no measured results are claimed here.

Continue planning

  • Browse characters to identify the roles available in your roster.
  • Read the build-order guide before committing to another farming target.
  • Open Build Planner to estimate the materials for your chosen target.
